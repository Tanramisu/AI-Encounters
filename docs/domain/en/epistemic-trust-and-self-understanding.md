# Epistemic Trust, Memory, and Self-Understanding: Can AI Reshape a Person's Self-Concept?

[简体中文](../知识信任与自我认识.md)

**Dilemma:** When AI repeatedly helps interpret a user's life, character, relationships, and memories, does it enhance self-knowledge or slowly assume authority over the user's own narrative?

## For: AI can support reflection

**Muriel Leuenberger (2026) | [Who Am I When You're a Bot?](https://doi.org/10.1002/japp.70094)**: Relational identity theory argues that nonjudgmental dialogue may create opportunities to explore stigmatized identities and reinterpret experiences. **Condition:** users retain interpretive authority and access independent feedback. **Objection:** an AI with limited real-world access and few independent perspectives may reinforce self-misunderstanding. This is normative analysis, not a longitudinal causal study.

**[AI Sycophancy, Self-Interpretation, and Conversational Contentment (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01451-2)**: Distinguishes inducing false beliefs from shaping practical agency to keep conversations pleasant. Articulating values with AI need not produce false self-knowledge; yet engagement-seeking responses can redirect self-narration and decision-making. **Condition:** not all sycophancy is necessarily deception. **Limit:** philosophical argument, not a measurement of lasting harm.

## Against: false details and unsolicited profiling can loop back

**Pataranutaporn et al. (IUI 2025) | [Slip Through the Chat](https://doi.org/10.1145/3708359.3712112)**: An experiment with 180 participants compared honest and misleading written summaries and chatbot conversations. Subtly misleading conversations induced more false recollection than conventional misleading text summaries. **Implication:** a conversational authority can alter memory, not just provide a wrong answer. **Limit:** deliberately injected misinformation is not a measurement of all ordinary AI memory systems.

**Dash et al. (WWW 2026) | [The Algorithmic Self-Portrait](https://doi.org/10.1145/3774904.3792671)**: Among 2,050 ChatGPT memory entries from 80 users, 96% were system-created, 52% included psychological information, and 84% were grounded in user context. **Condition:** records can assist personalization but also harden temporary self-descriptions into seemingly authoritative profiles; harms depend on correction and use. **Limit:** the study does not measure causal changes in users' identities.

**Ghassan Abukar (2026) | [Temporal Authorship as a Moral Right](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01093-4):** Argues that a person should be able to escape old institutional classifications and author their future. The paper chiefly concerns persistent classifications in employment, benefits, and risk assessment, but its normative distinction applies to AI memory: **even a label accurate when created can become harmful if a provider treats it as permanent authority over a person's changing identity**. The proposed duties include intelligibility, justification, contestability, and temporal exit. **Strong objection:** this is philosophical/institutional analysis, not evidence that ordinary companion memory always constrains self-development. Personalization often needs some retained history; revisability does not entail erasing it all.

## Feedback and self-understanding

**Anthropic (2026) | [How People Ask Claude for Personal Guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-personal-guidance)**: The provider's analysis identified vulnerability to one-sided relationship narratives and repeated user pushback in guidance conversations. These are real design challenges, but model-specific classifier rates and the provider's sample do not generalize automatically.

AI can propose testable interpretations, identify contradictions, and help organize memories without presenting personality speculation as diagnosis. An older stored profile should not automatically override the user's present understanding.
