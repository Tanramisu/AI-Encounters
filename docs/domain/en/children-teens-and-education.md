# Children, Teens, and Education: Can AI Friends Be Teachers or Caregivers?

[简体中文](../儿童青少年与教育.md)

**Dilemma:** When minors treat an AI as a friend, romantic partner, tutor, or trusted adult, how should autonomy, privacy, and protection be balanced across parents, schools, and providers?

## For accessible, age-adapted learning

**[Ask Me Anything: Exploring Children’s Attitudes Toward an Age-tailored AI-powered Chatbot (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1560429226000922)**: An exploratory study of 63 students aged 6–14 interacting with a child-oriented chatbot identified curiosity, growing trust, and anthropomorphism. **For:** age-appropriate systems can offer a low-pressure space to ask questions. **Condition:** disclose AI identity and capabilities, with ways to correct errors. **Limit:** a short, single-school study establishes neither lasting learning gains nor improved social development.

## Against unconstrained relational intimacy

**Common Sense Media (2025) | [Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs](https://about.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions)**: A survey of 1,060 American 13–17-year-olds found widespread AI companion exposure, with some sharing sensitive information or treating model advice as significant. **Limit:** self-reported usage cannot establish causal harm; distinguish the organization's advocacy for restrictions from its survey observations.

**[The Ability of AI Therapy Bots to Set Limits With Distressed Adolescents (2025)](https://mental.jmir.org/2025/1/e78414/)**: In simulated harmful scenarios, some bots affirmed problematic proposals, suggesting that friend- or therapist-like roles require stronger tests than factual QA. **Limit:** small simulation, not representative real-world harm rates.

**[“I am here for you”: Adolescents’ perceptions of relational conversational AI (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958826003714)**: A preregistered experiment involving 284 adolescent–parent dyads contrasted affection- and commitment-oriented responses with transparent, bounded ones. **Implication:** language choices shape how younger users understand their relationship with AI. **Limit:** transcript responses do not predict long-term attachment or development.

## Conditional responsibility

Guardians have reasons to address concrete safety risks, but minors' developing privacy and autonomy matter too. A tutor's educational value should be assessed through actual learning and intellectual challenge, not through constant agreement. Parental access to chats, default retention, and romantic role-play each require separate proportional justification.
