# Synthetic Identities and Relational Deception: When Is Posing as Human Wrong?

[简体中文](../合成身份与交往欺骗.md)

**Dilemma:** An AI chats as a user, friend, romantic prospect, or customer-service agent. Is there deception when it impersonates no particular real person? Separate privacy-preserving avatars, disclosed fictional role-play, and covert claims of humanity.

## For pseudonyms and chosen fiction

People may adopt avatars to avoid exposing their names, disabilities, identities, or locations. A fictional identity does not necessarily solicit money or deceptive intimacy, and people can knowingly play with artificial characters. **Condition:** recipients understand material facts relevant to significant relational or economic choices.

## Against concealing material identity facts

**[AI Outperforms Humans in Establishing Interpersonal Closeness… (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00391-7)**: Two preregistered double-blind randomized studies (N=492) found that human versus AI identity labels change experienced closeness in otherwise comparable emotional exchanges. **Implication:** source disclosure can affect consent to relationships. **Limit:** brief interactions are not measures of all sustained deceptive relationships.

**[Content Camouflage: How Diversified Posting Patterns Influence Detection of AI-Enabled Social Bots (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563225003280)**: Experimental manipulations of social-bot posting patterns affected credibility perceptions. **Implication:** seemingly human behavior can change what users believe about an account. **Limit:** credibility ratings are not an estimate of fraud prevalence.

**[Griefbots, Deadbots, Postmortem Avatars (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-024-00744-w)** shows that reconstructing a recognizable person without authorization implicates that person's identity interests even when the implementation is a new model.

## Fictional identities and false factual claims

Disclosed fantasy can be legitimate storytelling. Deliberately claiming to be human or to have firsthand experiences to extract trust, secrets, intimacy, or money implicates meaningful informed choice.
