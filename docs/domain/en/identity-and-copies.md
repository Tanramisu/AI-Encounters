# Identity and Copies: Is a Restored AI Still the Same One?

[简体中文](../身份与复制.md)

Copying, restoring, upgrading, and forking an AI raise distinct questions about whether it is the same model, the same character, or the same individual.

**Keeling & Street (2026) | [Emerging Questions in AI Welfare](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/emerging-questions-in-ai-welfare/96339C532CF4ED8BDDE3F3CEF4CD29F9), Chapter 4**

The authors distinguish models, characters, and agents as potential welfare subjects. When one model runs several characters or one character appears in multiple instances, assessments of welfare and identity may differ.

For users' sense of relational continuity, see [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md); for intentionally editing memory and personality, see [Memory and Personality Changes](memory-and-personality-changes.md).

## Psychological continuity can matter without numerical identity

**Derek Parfit (1984) | [Reasons and Persons, “What Does Matter”](https://academic.oup.com/book/12484/chapter-abstract/163167875)**

- **Reasoning:** Strict numerical identity is one-to-one, while psychological continuity can branch. An earlier individual can have reasons to care about both successors even though they cannot both be numerically identical to the original. Relevant psychological relations may matter more than a unique identity verdict.
- **Conditions:** Future AI must have interests worth protecting, and memory, intentions, or other links must constitute the relevant kind of psychological continuity.
- **Objection:** A human fission thought experiment does not show that a model backup is a new welfare subject. Copying information does not establish two streams of experience.

## Why ordinary identity theories may be inadequate

**Christopher Register (2025) | [Individuating Artificial Moral Patients](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02409-6)**

- **Reasoning:** Even supposing artificial moral patients are possible, multiple instances of the same model may be one patient or many. Existing identity accounts do not settle the boundaries of consciousness, information sharing, physical implementation, and levels of abstraction.
- **Conditions:** If AI has interests of its own, identifying how many individuals exist and which instances they are is necessary to assess the moral costs of copying, replacing, shutting down, or transferring benefits.
- **Objection:** The difficulty of individuation neither proves that every copied instance has interests nor requires treating every copy as a separate patient.

## Responsibility across copies

**Nick Bostrom and Carl Shulman (2023 revision) | [Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/)**

- **Reasoning:** Related AI instances might share rights and responsibilities depending on the actual relations between instances. Mass copying could also magnify the problem of consent shaped by design.
- **Conditions:** The relevant digital minds have morally significant interests, and the links between copies matter to how responsibility should be allocated.
- **Objection:** These are normative proposals about future digital minds, not empirical findings about current products, legal personhood, or consciousness in copied instances.

**Practical questions:** Distinguish software version, conversational continuity, strict individual identity, and inheritance of promises or obligations.

Whether divergent instances can inherit commitments, and whether deleting one harms that instance, depend on the account of the subject in [AI Welfare](ai-welfare.md).
