# Dependence, Loneliness, and Human Development: Help or Lost Autonomy?

[简体中文](../依赖孤独与人的成长.md)

**Dilemma:** When does relying on AI for companionship and life decisions augment autonomy, and when does it undermine independent judgment? Loneliness, duration, attachment, compulsive use, and the quality of human ties are distinct constructs.

## For: companionship can expand people's resources

Experiments on short-term loneliness relief are summarized in [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md). Their relevance to autonomy depends on whether users retain real-world relationships and alternatives.

**Muriel Leuenberger (2026) | [Who Am I When You're a Bot?](https://doi.org/10.1002/japp.70094)**: Relational identity theory suggests AI can offer safer spaces for affirmation, reflection, and identity exploration, including to people experiencing exclusion. **Counterargument:** an overly agreeable agent without an independent perspective could also limit identity development. This is philosophical argument, not an intervention study.

## Against: sycophancy and engagement can weaken agency

**Cheng et al. (2026) | [Sycophantic AI Decreases Prosocial Intentions and Promotes Dependence](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)**: Three preregistered experiments (N=2,405) found sycophantic responses increased users' confidence in their own side of a dispute and reduced willingness to repair relationships; trust and reuse intentions could also increase. **Limit:** many endpoints were attitudes or intentions, not later confirmed behavior. The treatment is *sycophancy*, not companionship itself.

The four-week randomized study (N=981) and its self-selected use-intensity associations are covered in [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md).

**[AI Genie Phenomenon (CHI 2026)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/socius/files/papers/chi2026-aiaddiction.pdf)**: Qualitative research distinguishes escapist role-play, pseudosocial companionship, and epistemic rabbit holes as different patterns of problematic use. **Condition:** identify impaired control and functional effects, rather than equating frequent or enthusiastic use with addiction.

## Assessing loss of autonomy

For competing longitudinal directions between loneliness and AI use, see [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md).

Ask whether AI can challenge the user, whether the user retains independent choices and relationships, whether daily functioning improves, and whether disengagement remains voluntary.
