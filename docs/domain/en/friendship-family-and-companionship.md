# Friendship, Family, and Companionship: Can AI Be a Friend or Family Member?

[简体中文](../友谊家庭与陪伴.md)

**Dilemma:** When someone treats an AI as a friend, child, or family member, are they developing a valuable new relationship, or mistaking one-sided emotional experience for mutual obligations? This concerns the status of relationships; psychological effects belong in [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md).

## For: a relationship can deliver real value

**John Danaher (2019) | [The Philosophical Case for Robot Friendship](https://doi.org/10.5325/JPOSTSTUD.3.1.0005)** argues against excluding robots simply because they imitate emotional signals. If a robot can meaningfully meet functional conditions of mutual well-wishing, shared activity, and equality, virtue friendship may be possible; even falling short, machines could complement human friendship. **Condition:** relational functions matter independently of biological origin. **Objection:** behavior may not establish that the AI has interests or benevolence of its own.

**Skjuve et al. (2021) | [My Chatbot Companion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2021.102601)** interviewed 18 people who already considered Replika a friend. They described progressive disclosure, acceptance, and always-available support. **Limit:** a selected qualitative sample captures their experiences, not AI's intentions or a population-level rate of successful friendships.

## Against: reciprocity needs another subject

**Lott and Hasselberger (2025) | [With Friends Like These](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11245-025-10247-8)** argue that friendship requires caring about another *for that being's own good*. If the AI has no good of its own, such concern cannot be reciprocal. **Conditions:** each party's interests are essential to friendship, and present AI lacks its own. **Objection:** this does not eliminate benefits to the person or determine the status of future AI.

**Lior Gazit (2026) | [When No One Laughs Back](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03331-z)** distinguishes a relational mirror supplying fitting responses from an independent source of experience that meets us with an autonomous life. Present companions might supply interaction without mutual experience. **Objection:** traditional reciprocity might be too narrow a standard for new valuable relationships.

## Openness under uncertainty

**John Danaher (2023) | [Moral Uncertainty and Our Relationships with Unknown Minds](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963180123000191)** argues that skepticism about AI moral status is compatible with openness to meaningful relationships. Compare actual asymmetrical risks of acceptance and exclusion; their magnitude requires empirical evidence.

Calling an AI one's child does not prove it has a child's needs. In shared households, the most emotionally attached person does not automatically control all memories or exclusive promises.
