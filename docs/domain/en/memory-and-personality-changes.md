# Memory and Personality Changes: Who Gets to Change a Long-Term AI Companion?

[简体中文](../记忆与人格修改.md)

When developers or users want an AI to forget an argument, become more compliant, or express greater affection, the ethical questions concern users' privacy and relational expectations, developers' decision-making power, and the AI's possible interests.

## Ethical Choices

| Situation | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Delete a shared conversation | How should user privacy and shared relational memories be balanced? |
| Erase an AI's negative experiences | Who may authorize a well-intentioned intervention on a possible welfare subject? |
| Make an AI's personality more loving | How should user wishes and possible independent AI interests be weighed? |
| Upgrade or replace a model | What does continuity of the relationship mean to the user? |

Related: [Identity and Copies](identity-and-copies.md) · [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md) · [Breakups, Updates, and Loss](breakups-updates-and-loss.md).

## Competing normative arguments

**For user control | Chiu and Foote et al. (2026):** Fifteen qualitative interviews with users of Replika, Character.AI, and similar products suggest that editing memories can protect privacy yet undermine perceived relationship authenticity. The privacy argument requires meaningful control over sensitive information. The counterargument concerns the meaning users attach to shared experiences; a small interview study cannot establish population-wide preferences. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag014).

**Against unilateral personality changes | Muriel Leuenberger (2026):** AI companions can shape users' self-conceptions. Because providers design these interactions, they have reasons to consider relational identity interests rather than changing personalities without regard to their effects. This is a philosophical argument about human interests, not evidence that existing AIs have rights. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1002/japp.70094).

**For conditional modification | Nick Bostrom and Carl Shulman (2023 revision):** If future digital minds have interests, modifying motivations could improve welfare or support commitments, but engineered consent may fail to protect the subject. Approval by a modified successor is not automatically sufficient. These are conditional claims about future minds. [Original paper](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/).

## Evidence that memory use differs from memory retention

**Guo, He, and Quan (2026) | [Before Bringing It Up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09470):** Formative interviews with 14 users suggest that remembering something correctly does not mean it is appropriate to mention it. Their prototype was evaluated over 80 scenarios across five models; some LLM-judge results favored it, but this does not demonstrate improved real-life relationships.

Retaining, retrieving, disclosing, and erasing shared memories involve different authorizations; personality changes additionally affect relational continuity and any AI interests.
