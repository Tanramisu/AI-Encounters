# Human–AI Companionship: How Does It Affect People?

[简体中文](../人机亲密关系.md)

Experiments, longitudinal surveys, and interviews examine loneliness, attachment, privacy, social relationships, and the loss of AI companions.

## Observational Findings on Sustained Use


- **Fang et al. (2025) | [How AI and Human Behaviors Shape Psychosocial Effects of Chatbot Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473)**: A four-week randomized study involving 981 people. Overall effects of interaction modes and topics were inconsistent; actual time spent using chatbots was associated with some adverse psychological outcomes.

- **Zhang et al. (2026) | [Interaction with AI Companions and Psychological Well-Being](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02516-2)**: An observational study of 1,131 adult Character.AI users. Among people with smaller social networks, intensive use and greater self-disclosure, some patterns of companion use were associated with lower well-being.

## Relationships, Privacy, and Disruption

- For platform control of shared memories, see [Relationship Infrastructure and Platform Power](relationship-infrastructure-and-platform-power.md).
- For loss following service changes, see [Breakups, Updates, and Loss](breakups-updates-and-loss.md).
- **[*Potential and Pitfalls of Romantic AI Companions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100715) (2025)**: Synthesizes 23 studies on emotional support, dependence, manipulation, privacy, and relationship disruptions.
- **Vowels et al. (2026) | [From Friends to Lovers](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-025-03375-0)**: In a mixed-methods study of 135 people, 55.6% reported hesitation or resistance toward deep emotional relationships with AI; ratings varied with the research setting.

## Experiments and longitudinal outcomes

**De Freitas et al. (2025 online; 2026 issue):** [Experiments](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf040) show reductions in momentary loneliness, including over a week, especially when users feel heard. This cannot establish long-term gains.

**Folk and Dunn (2026):** A [12-month study](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261427747) of over 2,000 adults in four countries found that chatbot use predicted increased loneliness on a single-item measure. On a broader measure, low social connection predicted later chatbot use; use did not predict declining connection. These exploratory associations do not determine causal direction.

**Ibrahim et al. (2026 preprint):** [Five preregistered studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07912) included 3,075 participants and 12,766 conversations. A three-week randomized study found lower satisfaction with real-world interactions after repeated use of *sycophantic* AI, despite immediate feelings of understanding. Results concern this response style, not every companion AI.



**Zhang et al. (2026 preprint):** A [two-wave study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07243) surveyed 1,182 Character.AI users and followed up with 439 roughly one year later. Sustained engagement was associated with lower well-being, partly explained by reduced in-person interaction. Attrition and self-selected usage limit causal conclusions.
