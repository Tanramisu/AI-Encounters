# AI-Mediated Human Relationships: Is Ghostwritten Affection Deceptive?

[简体中文](../AI介入人与人的关系.md)

**Dilemma:** Does AI-assisted writing of apologies, breakups, or dating messages help people express what they mean, or outsource sincerity and disclose someone else's private data without consent?

## For assistance with expression

AI may help people facing language barriers, strong emotion, or writing difficulty communicate their real intentions. **Conditions:** the sender reviews, endorses, and assumes responsibility for the message; AI is an aid, not an undisclosed person claiming an independent relationship. Sincerity does not logically require typing every word oneself.

## Against concealed emotional ghostwriting

**[AI Ghostwriting Remorse (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1002/cb.70057)**: Experiments find guilt when people present AI-generated emotionally meaningful messages as solely self-authored; concealing a friend's ghostwriting showed a similar effect, while using a printed greeting card did not. **Explanation:** concealment and authorship norms, rather than AI alone, may drive the concern. **Limit:** felt guilt does not establish actual harm to every recipient or a universal duty to disclose all AI assistance.

**[The Illusion of Empathy (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1568911)**: Experiments rating emotionally supportive texts found decreased perceived authenticity after disclosure of AI authorship. **Limit:** ratings on particular messages do not establish that every real-world relationship is harmed.

## Others' secrets and consent

**Chiu and Foote et al. (2026) | [Chatting with Confidants or Corporations?](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag014)**: Qualitative interviews illuminate the gap between perceived confidant intimacy and corporate data control. **Normative implication:** uploading another person's messages, health disclosures, or purported infidelity evidence can implicate their interests, regardless of the user's wish for advice. The study does not estimate ghostwriting harm prevalence.

**Direct relational outcomes | Levkovich and Alon (2026) | [Generative AI as a Third Voice in Human Couple Relationships](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101255):** A JBI/PRISMA systematic review of **21 studies from 11 countries across seven searched databases** investigated couple advice, conflict mediation, and AI-assisted communication. Users often experienced AI as empathic and some interventions reported short-term gains in satisfaction or communication, yet **the strongest controlled trial did not outperform an established reflective writing exercise**. **Condition:** measure benefits relative to realistic alternatives, not simply before/after satisfaction. **Limit:** heterogeneous designs and endpoints do not establish durable relationship improvement, nor do they prove all AI assistance ineffective.

## Conditional assessment

Distinguish: editing a sender's actual views from secretly maintaining an entire relationship on their behalf; whether the recipient is materially misled about who is speaking; private third-party information; and the sender's ability to explain and own the message. Automated dating, delegated breakup, or presenting an AI as a real person raise stronger disclosure and responsibility concerns than spelling assistance.
