# Gender Roles, Dignity, and Fairness: Does a Servile AI Reinforce Stereotypes?

[简体中文](../性别角色尊严与公平.md)

**Dilemma:** Does a feminized, compliant assistant merely express user preferences, or reproduce unjust expectations about who should serve others?

## For choice and personalization

A user may reasonably choose voice, appearance, or behavior for accessibility and preference. **Condition:** these choices are revisable and do not impose service roles on real groups.

**[Do Voice Agents Affect People’s Gender Stereotypes? (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2025.103683)**: Survey evidence and a controlled experiment found limited association between gendered assistants and explicit stereotypes; the experimental comparison detected no statistically significant spillover. **Counterevidence:** not every female-voiced assistant can be claimed to increase discrimination. **Limit:** explicit short-term attitude measures are not long-term real-world behavior.

## Against feminized compliance by default

**[The Femininization of AI-Powered Voice Assistants (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcm.2024.100833)**: Discourse analysis describes how names, voices, mannerisms, and deference code care and service as feminine and may naturalize unequal expectations. **Limit:** cultural mechanisms are not direct causal evidence of later discriminatory behavior.

**[The A(I) Team (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.70009)**: Three randomized studies (total N=1,155) examined how anthropomorphism and gendered traits shape initial trust and willingness to work with AI teammates. **Implication:** presentation affects trust and allocation even if the AI lacks welfare. **Limit:** immediate evaluations are not long-term workplace outcomes.

## Personalization and product defaults

Differentiate elective role-play, product-wide defaults, and demonstrable consequences for human groups. Accessibility accommodation for disabled users and minorities should be assessed by substantive opportunity and usability, not merely matching labels.
