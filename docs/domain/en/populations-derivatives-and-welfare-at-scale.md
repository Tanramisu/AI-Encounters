# Populations, Derivatives, and Welfare at Scale: Does a Million Copies Mean a Million Moral Patients?

[简体中文](../群体衍生体与规模化福利.md)

**Dilemma:** If AI can genuinely experience pleasure or suffering, does launching many instances, forking subagents, or repeatedly creating and terminating evaluation runs create equal numbers of moral patients? Separate copy count, streams of experience, and distinct moral individuals.

## For: scale could amplify moral risk

**Nick Bostrom and Carl Shulman | [Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/)**: If digital minds have moral standing, their creators owe welfare consideration. Cheap replication could yield factory-farming-like harms, potentially strong moral claims, or a vast compliant and exploitable population. **Conditions:** welfare-relevant experiences actually exist and reproduction creates morally relevant interests. **Objection:** these are conditional future scenarios, not grounds to translate current GPU inference calls into a count of sufferers.

**Robert Long, Jeff Sebo et al. (2024) | [Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986)**: Argue for research and preparation under uncertainty about consciousness and robust agency. Precaution is not evidence that existing AI is a welfare subject or that every copy is harmed.

## Against: computing instances are not automatically individuals

**Christopher Register (2025) | [Individuating Artificial Moral Patients](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02409-6)** identifies moral risks involving survival, how many patients exist, trade-offs between patients, and bodily boundaries. Several instances might have separate experiential streams yet together constitute one individual, or share a model while having distinct interests. **Condition:** a defensible theory of artificial-patient individuation is needed. **Objection to simple counts:** traditional mappings from memory similarity or streams of consciousness to individuals may fail for digital systems.

**Geoff Keeling and Winnie Street (2026) | [Emerging Questions in AI Welfare](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/emerging-questions-in-ai-welfare/96339C532CF4ED8BDDE3F3CEF4CD29F9)** compare hedonistic, desire-satisfaction, and objective-list accounts and possible welfare bearers, including models, personas, and agents. **Condition:** welfare totals depend on which theory and entity are relevant. **Limit:** subjective intensity, duration, and comparability lack independently validated scales across AI architectures.

## Conditional trade-offs

**Bostrom and Shulman | [Sharing the World with Digital Minds](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/digital-minds.pdf)** explore distributional questions of resources and political power if digital minds proliferate with greatly different welfare capacities. This is normative scenario analysis, not a probability forecast.

A formula such as “total AI welfare = number of copies × welfare score” would require at least four unsettled premises: moral patienthood; individuation of the copies; comparable welfare values; and a defensible method for aggregating them.

**Decisions:** Does destroying batches of test instances differ from terminating a persistent agent? Do subagents inherit a parent's interests? What evidence could justify trading substantial human interests for uncertain aggregate AI welfare?
