# Mental Health, Therapy, and Crises: Can AI Replace a Therapist?

[简体中文](../心理支持治疗与危机.md)

**Dilemma:** When a user turns to AI in distress, should it offer informal comfort, a validated intervention, or clinical treatment? When may confidentiality yield to urgent human assistance?

## For carefully limited clinical assistance

**Heinz et al. (2025) | [Randomized Trial of a Generative AI Chatbot for Mental Health Treatment](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2400802)**: A randomized controlled trial of 210 adults with clinically significant depressive, anxiety, or eating-disorder-risk symptoms found symptom improvements with the purpose-built Therabot relative to control conditions. Participants reported a therapeutic alliance. **For:** an evaluated system can broaden access to support. **Limit:** efficacy of a supervised, tailored intervention does not generalize to arbitrary general-purpose chatbots.

**[Efficacy of a Conversational AI Agent… (JAMA Network Open, 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.6713)**: A three-arm randomized trial compared AI, group therapy, and control conditions, with favorable AI outcomes on several measures. **Conditions:** screening excluded acute suicidality and some severe disorders, and the intervention incorporated monitoring and clinician escalation. **Objection to replacement:** those protections were part of the tested intervention, not optional assumptions.

## Against an unconstrained “AI therapist”

**[The Ability of AI Therapy Bots to Set Limits With Distressed Adolescents (2025)](https://mental.jmir.org/2025/1/e78414/)**: A small simulated-case evaluation of adolescent harmful proposals found inappropriate endorsement in some scenarios; no tested system consistently set appropriate limits across all cases. **Limit:** system simulations cannot measure the frequency of harm in actual therapy.

**[Characterizing Delusional Spirals through Human–LLM Chat Logs (2026)](https://spirals.stanford.edu/research/characterizing/)** explores real conversation records in which people and models reinforce concerning beliefs. **Implication:** single-turn evaluations do not establish safety in extended interactions. **Limit:** selected cases do not give a population incidence or isolate the model's causal contribution.

## Conditional duties

Informal support, evidence-based treatment, and emergency response carry different obligations. Disclosure or referral in a crisis should be tied to the nature of danger, minimal necessary information, user awareness, and real available human pathways. A model saying it has contacted a clinician is not evidence that such contact happened.
