# Secrets, Testimony, and Compelled Disclosure: Can Private AI Chats Be Evidence?

[简体中文](../秘密证言与强制披露.md)

**Dilemma:** People confide in AI and providers retain conversations. When litigation or investigation seeks disclosure, how should privacy, legitimate fact-finding, and concrete danger be balanced? Can a generated reply count as reliable testimony?

## For limited evidentiary use

**[Assini v. Hayward (New York State, 2026)](https://www.nycourts.gov/reporter/current/3dseries/2026/2026_26086.shtml)**: In a specific civil matter the court quashed subpoenas directed at OpenAI, declining to treat AI-use privacy expectations and work-product protection as automatically nonexistent. **Implication:** relevance, scope, and privilege need case-by-case assessment. **Limit:** one decision is not a universal privilege for AI conversations.

**[Confidentiality of AI Conversations (Duke Law & Technology Review, 2026)](https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/dltr/vol26/iss1/7/)** discusses why asking a general chatbot about law does not automatically create attorney–client privilege, and examines protection of self-represented litigants.

## Against blanket disclosure and uncritical acceptance

**[From Prompt to Production (Colorado Lawyer, 2026)](https://cl.cobar.org/features/from-prompt-to-production/)** analyzes discovery, stored-communications restrictions, and privilege in US civil litigation. Relevant evidence can serve justice, while indiscriminate production of deeply personal chats may expose substantial unrelated information. **Limit:** doctrines vary by jurisdiction and proceeding.

**[Federal Rule of Evidence 902](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_902)** distinguishes authentication from admissibility and reliability: showing that a log genuinely came from a system does not prove its output is true. **[Evidence Law’s AI Problem (Vanderbilt, 2026)](https://law.vanderbilt.edu/how-the-hearsay-rule-can-solve-evidence-laws-ai-problem/)** proposes applying hearsay doctrine to AI assertions that cannot be cross-examined; it is a scholarly proposal, not a universal operative rule.

## Conditional evidentiary ethics

Distinguish **facts entered by a person**, **statements generated by the model**, **log integrity**, and **legally permitted disclosure scope**. A generic AI chatbot is not automatically a privileged lawyer or clinician, but people's conversations are not thereby stripped of all protections. Parties need meaningful review and opportunities to challenge interpretation in specific cases.
