# AI Coworkers and Co-Creation: Who Deserves Trust, Credit, and Recognition?

[简体中文](../AI同事与共同创作.md)

**Dilemma:** After collaborating with AI over time, should we treat it as a teammate? Who deserves attribution, payment, and recognition: users, prompt writers, developers, training-data contributors, or AI?

## For accounting for different contributions

**[Which Contributions Deserve Credit? (CHI 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18357)**: A survey of 155 knowledge workers found that perceived deserved credit varied with contribution type, amount, and initiative. **Implication:** a binary AI-used label poorly describes actual work. **Limit:** perceptions are neither copyright law nor evidence of AI moral standing.

**[Engaging the Many-Hands Problem of Generative-AI Outputs (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00440-7)** offers an analytic framework for distinguishing contributions by human workers and automated systems across training, development, and the creative process. **Objection:** contributing is not sufficient to establish identical legal authorship rights.

## Against treating AI teammates as independently responsible

**[Trusting Autonomous Teammates in Human–AI Teams (CHI 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713527)**: A literature review describes how reliability, transparency, and contextual factors shape trust in AI teammates. **Implication:** the teammate framing can aid collaboration without establishing AI welfare or legal accountability.

**[The Delegation Illusion (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01383-x)**: Human deployers can remain answerable for delegation despite agent autonomy. An AI's objection may flag a relevant moral risk without being an independent moral patient's protest.

## Conditional attribution

Distinguish **factual contribution**, **professional recognition**, **enforceable legal rights**, and **possible AI welfare**. Fair credit does not require listing every model in every work, and “AI colleague” rhetoric must not hide the labor of people involved.
