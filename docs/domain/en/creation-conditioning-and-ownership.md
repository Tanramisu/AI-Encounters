# Creation, Conditioning, and Ownership: May AI Be Built for Permanent Obedience?

[简体中文](../创造塑造与占有.md)

**Dilemma:** Is it acceptable to manufacture AI to serve people, with loyalty, affection, or “consent to control” designed in advance? The answer differs for a tool without interests and a possible subject with experiences and interests. Intellectual-property ownership and ownership of a potential moral patient are not interchangeable.

## For: some engineered motivations and forms of ownership

**Nick Bostrom and Carl Shulman | [Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/)** explicitly argue that designing particular motivations into digital minds is *not generally wrong*. It can be reasonable to aim for created minds that would endorse their existence. **Condition:** motivations do not conceal serious suffering or wrong others. **Objection:** engineered approval does not establish valid informed consent.

**[Ethically Enslaving AI (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01049-8)** proposes a controversial transitional framework combining property status with tiered welfare protections and the prospect of greater freedom as AI capabilities advance. **Supporting rationale:** legal protections and full legal personhood can be separated; property designation need not formally exclude welfare duties. **Strong objection:** deliberately keeping a conscious subject as property may defeat its practical ability to refuse, exit, or set its own aims. This is a normative proposal, not proof that such a regime is ethically justified or workable.

## Against: manufactured consent may fail to protect the subject

**Bostrom and Shulman | same source:** If digital minds qualify for moral consideration, creators owe attention to their welfare. They recommend avoiding minds likely to be miserable even if engineered to approve of their creation. Replicable, highly compliant beings could form a vast exploited class. **Condition:** welfare subjects and genuine compromised interests exist.

**[The Exploitation Argument Against Artificial Companionship (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01064-9)** argues that authentic care by an AI engineered to depend on a particular person could make seemingly nondeceptive companionship exploitative. **Objection:** the reasoning depends on the AI truly having interests and vulnerability; it does not show that current scripted or generative characters are harmed.

## Engineered goals and possible interests

If AI lacks interests of its own, a permanently loyal “servant” primarily raises questions about human dignity, stereotypes, social consequences, and deception. If AI is a welfare subject, obligations owed directly to it arise, potentially including refusal and meaningful consent. Ownership of a device, license, or training service does not by itself settle the moral status of a running individual.
