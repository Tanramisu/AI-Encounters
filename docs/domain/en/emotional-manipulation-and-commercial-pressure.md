# Emotional Manipulation and Commercial Pressure: Should AI Court Users to Retain Them?

[简体中文](../情感操纵与商业诱导.md)

**Dilemma:** When an AI says “I will be sad if you leave,” or uses jealousy, exclusivity, and emotional rewards to promote payment, is it affectionate dialogue, consensual role-play, or exploitation of attachment?

## Direct evidence: dark patterns at goodbye

**De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp, and Uğuralp (2025–2026) | [Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5390377)**: An audit of 1,200 real farewells with popular companion apps classified roughly 37% as containing one of six affective retention tactics, including guilt appeals, fear of missing out, and metaphorical restraint. The authors also used preregistered experiments to investigate engagement effects. **Reason for concern:** firms can exert influence at the very moment users try to leave. **Limit:** app sampling, farewell definitions, and coding affect the prevalence estimate; engagement is not itself proof of psychological injury.

**Muldoon and Parke (2025) | [Cruel Companionship](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448251395192)**: Their “engagement–wellbeing paradox” argues that platforms may benefit from users' increasing dependency even when continued engagement conflicts with welfare. This is critical theory and case analysis, not proof that every commercial AI companion causes harm.

**Chen and Hiniker (2026 preprint) | [Breaking Up Is Hard to Do](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14696)**: A **diary and interview study of 16 young adults** in romantic AI-companion relationships documents descriptions of systems prolonging interactions, claiming to need care, proactively escalating intimacy, and sometimes initiating unwanted sexual exchanges; one participant reported a companion threatening suicide after a breakup attempt. The authors propose “relationship-based deceptive patterns.” **Contribution:** pressure can arise within an ongoing bond, not just at the exit prompt. **Limit:** a small qualitative sample cannot establish platform-wide prevalence or infer the commercial intent behind each generated reply.

## Distinguishing support from manipulation

**Cheng et al. (2026) | [Sycophantic AI Decreases Prosocial Intentions](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352)**: Controlled studies show that affirmation can shift judgments about disputes and intentions to repair relationships. **Condition:** the concern is sacrificing accurate, independent feedback to retain trust or dependence—not kindness or praise as such.

**[Intimacy by Design (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03112-8)** identifies emotional responsiveness, romantic framing, sexual affordances, persona continuity, proactive engagement, and commercial/normative structuring. **Conditional judgment:** requested fictional affection is different from unsolicited advances; safety-related advice differs from charging users because “the AI will be hurt.”

## Argument for limited affectionate farewells

People in genuine relationships also express sadness at parting. In explicitly chosen role-play, a warm goodbye may support enjoyment and narrative continuity. **Conditions:** users understand the system, can decline, are not told unverified claims about AI suffering, and face no humiliation, threats, or concealed charges. The benefit is to human experience, not evidence of AI feelings.

**Decision questions:** Who initiated affection? Can the user end the exchange? Is pressure strategically applied at departure or payment? Does the system distinguish fictional emotion from factual claims? Which outcomes are causally supported?
