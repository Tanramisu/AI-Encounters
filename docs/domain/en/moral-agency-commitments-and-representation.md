# Moral Agency, Commitments, and Representation: Can AI Bear Responsibility?

[简体中文](../道德代理承诺与代表.md)

**Dilemma:** An AI agent promises to help and then harms a third party. Is the AI morally accountable, and who can represent it? Distinguish *acting on morally significant matters*, *making reliable commitments*, *being blameworthy*, and *being a legal person*.

## For: limited artificial moral agency

**Herman Veluwenkamp (2024) | [Artificial Agents: Responsibility & Control Gaps](https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174X.2024.2410995)** distinguishes responsibility gaps from gaps in meaningful control and discusses strengthening AI moral agency alongside human-control alternatives. **Reasoning:** autonomous effects on others require serious moral analysis beyond simply saying “it is a tool.” **Objection:** technical autonomy is not equivalent to grasping reasons or deserving blame.

**[Artificial Moral Characters (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01033-2)** interprets constitutional AI through virtue and embodied/situated cognition, suggesting engineered stable moral dispositions can play a normative role in joint human–AI action. **Condition:** “character” may describe trained tendencies without demonstrating independent moral personhood.

## Against: delegated action does not itself create responsibility

**[Normativity and AI Moral Agency (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00566-8)** criticizes minimalist concepts that equate autonomous output or ethical advice with moral agency, because human moral agency also involves normative participation in practices of reasons and answerability. **Condition:** agency requires richer capacities than performance. **Objection:** an excessively human-centered definition could exclude genuinely novel future agents by stipulation.

**[The Delegation Illusion (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01383-x)** separates attributability, answerability, and accountability. An identifiable principal who releases an agent into a foreseeably risky domain can remain answerable for that delegation despite remoteness from specific outputs. **Scope:** voluntary, identifiable deployment with foreseeable types of harm; it does not settle whether AI could independently bear responsibility.

## Conditional promises and representation

An AI may operationally fulfill promises while lacking the moral standing to make them *in its own name*. Execution reliability and moral answerability are different. If AI has welfare-relevant interests, representatives may be necessary, yet developers, owners, and close users may have conflicts of interest.

**Bostrom and Shulman | [Propositions Concerning Digital Minds and Society](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/propositions-concerning-digital-minds-and-society/)** suggest large AI organizations designate people to consider and, when appropriate, represent possible digital minds. This is a precautionary governance proposal, not proof of current AI personhood.

**Keep distinct:** delegated instrument, independent actor, potential welfare subject, and represented population.
