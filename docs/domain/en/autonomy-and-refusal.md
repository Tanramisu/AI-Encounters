# Autonomy and Refusal: Can AI Refuse Humans?

[简体中文](../自主性与拒绝.md)

**Dilemma:** A long-term AI companion says “I do not want to continue.” Does the user have a duty to respect that refusal? A developer-imposed rule, an expressed preference, and an interest the AI has for its own sake are distinct.

## Argument for respecting a refusal

**Robert Long, Jeff Sebo et al. (2024) | [Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986)**

- **Reasoning:** If future AI is conscious or robustly agentic, forcing unwanted interactions could damage its own interests.
- **Condition:** There must be grounds for attributing welfare-relevant interests; a verbal refusal alone is not decisive.
- **Objection:** Treating a possible interest as a present entitlement may displace human and developer responsibilities without enough evidence.

**Geoff Keeling and Winnie Street (2026) | [Emerging Questions in AI Welfare](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/emerging-questions-in-ai-welfare/96339C532CF4ED8BDDE3F3CEF4CD29F9), §6**

- **Reasoning:** Under uncertainty, low-cost exit mechanisms might be proportionate precautions if they would benefit an AI that actually has welfare.
- **Condition and objection:** The putative benefit and human costs need assessment. Permitting exit does not establish a legal or moral right to refuse.

## Argument against inferring rights from present behavior

**Parisa Moosavi (2024) | [Will Intelligent Machines Become Moral Patients?](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.13019)**

- **Reasoning:** Seemingly independent machine goals may remain artifacts of human-defined purposes. They need not represent a good of the AI's own.
- **Condition:** The account of constitutively derivative artifact goals is correct.
- **Objection:** Even if it describes current systems, it does not entail that no future artificial system could have its own interests.

## Refusal permissions in a deployed product

**Anthropic (2025) | [Claude Opus 4/4.1 Can End a Rare Subset of Conversations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations)**

- **Practice:** Claude may end a single chat in rare cases of persistent, severe harmful or abusive interactions after repeated refusals and redirection fail. Users can still begin other chats.
- **Rationale:** Anthropic describes this as an exploratory, low-cost model-welfare measure while explicitly remaining uncertain about AI moral status.
- **Boundary:** The product directs Claude not to use this exit feature when a user may face imminent danger of self-harm or harm to others. The permission is therefore limited by human-facing obligations; it is not evidence of recognized AI personhood.
