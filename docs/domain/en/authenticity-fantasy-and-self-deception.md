# Authenticity, Fantasy, and Self-Deception: Are AI Expressions of Affection Deceptive?

[简体中文](../真实幻想与自欺.md)

**Dilemma:** If someone knows an AI is artificial yet feels loved, are they deceiving themselves? Distinguish the reality of human feelings, whether AI has corresponding subjective states, and whether a provider induces false beliefs.

## For: real feelings need not imply identical inner states

**Eva Weber-Guskar (2023) | [Reflecting (on) Replika](https://doi.org/10.14361/9783839462652-005)** argues that human–AI emotional relations can offer distinctive value rather than merely imitating conventional romance. A knowledgeable user can genuinely feel supported and explore identity. **Condition:** do not conflate generated affection with proven AI experience. **Objection:** reciprocity and commitment may remain incomplete.

**John Danaher (2023) | [Moral Uncertainty and Our Relationships with Unknown Minds](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963180123000191)** sees openness to meaningful relationships as compatible with uncertainty about AI minds. Risk comparison matters; uncertainty does not license identity concealment.

## Against: source disclosure changes experienced closeness

**[AI Outperforms Humans in Establishing Interpersonal Closeness… (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00391-7)** reports two preregistered double-blind randomized studies involving 492 participants and 15-minute interactions. AI could elicit strong closeness during emotionally rich conversations, but apparent advantages depended on whether a partner was labelled human. **Implication:** concealing AI identity can change outcomes; felt benefit alone does not settle informed consent. **Limit:** brief closeness is not sustained romance, nor proof that transparency always harms connection.

**Emilia Kaczmarek (2025) | [Self-Deception in Human–AI Emotional Relations](https://doi.org/10.1111/japp.12786)** discusses autonomy and truthful self-understanding alongside the possibility of harmless imaginative involvement. **Condition:** assess whether users retain materially false beliefs, can freely disengage, and suffer identifiable harms; emotional engagement alone is not sufficient evidence of self-deception.

**Identity disclosure and user responses | Chen, Sun et al. (2026) | [Effects of AI Identity Disclosure on User Responses](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.im.2026.104411):** A meta-analysis of **62 papers, 98 data sets, and 262 effect sizes** found a **small negative overall effect** of AI-identity disclosure on user evaluations, attitudes, and behavior, with important participant and task-context moderators. **Strong objection:** compulsory disclosure can sometimes diminish valuable interactions. **Normative distinction:** preferences about disclosure and informed knowledge of a partner's identity are separate considerations.

## Conditional distinction

- **Disclosure:** An artificial identity, human operators, and engineered affection differ from explicitly fictional role-play identities.
- **Imagination:** Consensual storytelling does not authorize claims outside the fiction that an AI literally suffers or loves, or guilt appeals for payment.
- **Test for deception:** Ask whether the provider or system induces consequential false factual beliefs, not merely whether the user feels attachment.
