# Virtual Public Figures and Parasocial Influence: Can AI Influencers Claim Firsthand Experience?

[简体中文](../虚拟公众人物与拟社会影响.md)

**Dilemma:** AI influencers and virtual celebrities tell stories, endorse products, and form sustained fan attachments. Must audiences know who controls them and which claimed experiences are fictional?

## For virtual creative expression

**[Virtual Influencers vs. Human Influencers (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2024.2374100)**: In a 130-person online between-subject experiment, some virtual influencers were perceived as more authentic than human ones. **Implication:** virtual identity need not destroy audience value. **Limit:** perceived authenticity is not proof that product endorsements are accurate; sample and context matter.

**Barari, Eisend, and Kulczynski (2026) | [Virtual Influencer Marketing: A Meta-Analytic Review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01186-w)**: The meta-analysis finds that virtual influencers are **perceived as more novel and achieve broadly comparable consumer engagement, but underperform human influencers on credibility, advertising/brand attitudes, and behavioral intentions**. Anthropomorphism and fit with brand, product, and message explain some variation. **Ethical boundary:** engagement does not authenticate claims of firsthand product experience, but reduced average credibility does not make all fictional-persona relationships valueless.

## Against manufactured firsthand claims

**[Simulated Influence, Real Impacts (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-02889-y)**: Cross-cultural qualitative research examines emotional authenticity, social norms, and trust in virtual influencers. Fictional storytelling is distinct from claiming verified personal experience when affecting real purchasing decisions. **Limit:** qualitative evidence does not estimate financial harm rates.

**[Artificial Yet Credible (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115940)** distinguishes authenticity of the virtual persona from credibility of messages, product claims, and endorsed values. **Implication:** disclosing “virtual influencer” does not establish that every claim is true.

## Conditional disclosure and attribution

Support synthetic creative figures and voluntarily chosen parasocial ties while distinguishing fiction from product testing, advertiser control, and impersonation of real performers. Fan investment may justify explanations around major persona changes but does not automatically confer ownership. Public and political positions expressed by a character should remain attributable to those actually controlling and authorizing it.
