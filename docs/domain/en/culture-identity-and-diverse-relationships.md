# Culture, Identity, and Diverse Relationships: Whose Norms Shape AI Companionship?

[简体中文](../文化身份与差异化关系.md)

**Dilemma:** Which norms about families, gender, language, and faith should an AI companion, teacher, or adviser use? Cultural accommodation must be assessed against individual preferences, third-party interests, and evidence of actual differences.

## For plural relationships and accommodation

**[Cultural Variation in Attitudes Toward Social Chatbots (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220221251317950)**: Two studies (combined N=1,659) found differences in expectations of bonding with social chatbots between East Asian and European cultural-background samples, related in part to cultural experience. **Implication:** expectations about robot companionship and acceptability differ across populations. **Limit:** group-level attitudes do not establish cultural unanimity or individual welfare benefits.

For associations between offline support and AI-companion outcomes, see [Human–AI Companionship](human-ai-companionship.md). Access to other forms of support should be assessed for the users and communities in question.

**Hollis et al. (2026) | [AI Companionship and Loneliness in Autistic and Neurotypical Users](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101387)**: A preregistered **cross-sectional study of 365 autistic and neurotypical adults** found greater loneliness among autistic participants, but **companion-chatbot users were not lonelier than nonusers** and usage did not amplify the neurotype gap. **Implication:** selecting AI companionship is not by itself an indicator of withdrawal, especially for autistic users. **Limit:** self-selection and cross-sectional design cannot show that chatbots reduce loneliness or remedy barriers to human support.

**Williams et al. (2026) | [Randomized trial of the Purrble tactile companion robot](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04422-6)**: **153 LGBTQ+ young people with self-harm ideation** were assigned to Purrble plus safety planning versus safety planning alone. At 13 weeks, the combined group reported **fewer emotion regulation difficulties** (adjusted mean difference −3.04, 95% CI −4.92 to −1.16), and lower anxiety and depression symptoms; **the between-group main effect on self-harm was not significant**. **Implication:** specific underserved users can benefit from accessible supportive tools. **Limit:** Purrble is a *tactile calming robot*, not a conversational AI partner; both groups received structured safety planning, so this trial neither proves efficacy for chat companions nor that self-harm was prevented.

## Against imposing false cultural authority

For linguistic and doctrinal differences in religious-question benchmarks, see [Religion, Spirituality, and Meaning](religion-spirituality-and-meaning.md).

Using someone's language for accessibility, representing a fictional cultural role for learning, and falsely impersonating a real minority identity are distinct. **Objection to blanket restrictions:** forbidding style adaptation can itself reduce access to support.

## Individual accommodation and group stereotypes

Users should be able to set boundaries compatible with their age, language, communication abilities, and way of life, without using accommodation to justify harm to others. Cultural trends are variables to investigate, not fixed traits of every East Asian, Western, or minority user. Claims of fairness require evidence from the actual people and regions concerned.
