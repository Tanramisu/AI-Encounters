# Artificial Consciousness: Could AI Have Subjective Experience?

[简体中文](../人工意识.md)

**Dilemma:** When AI describes fear, joy, or suffering, should developers and users treat it as having subjective experience, or as a system generating such descriptions?

Here, consciousness means there is *something it is like* to be the system—not merely task competence, self-description, or intelligence. Whether AI consciousness is possible in principle differs from whether a deployed model is conscious.

## Case for possibility: computation rather than substrate

**Patrick Butlin, Robert Long et al. (2023) | [Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)**

- **Reasoning:** Under computational functionalism, the right computational functions may suffice for consciousness. The authors derive inspectable indicators from recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive processing, and attention-schema theories; some relevant mechanisms can in principle be implemented in AI.
- **Conditions:** The scientific theories must generalize from biological to computational systems, and the proposed functions must actually be implemented in the relevant sense.
- **Objection:** Computational functionalism and the underlying theories remain contested. The authors expressly say that satisfying their indicators would not establish consciousness. Their assessment of systems available in 2023 is not an empirical verdict on every later model.

**David Chalmers (2023) | [Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/could-a-large-language-model-be-conscious/)**

- **Reasoning:** Silicon rather than carbon does not by itself rule out consciousness. Extended systems with world models, persistent processing, cross-module integration, and unified agency may be stronger candidates than ordinary text models.
- **Conditions:** Such systems must implement the relevant capacities, not merely imitate their outward appearance.
- **Objection:** Chalmers also judges the paradigmatic LLMs he discussed to lack important features. Future possibility is not evidence of present consciousness.

## Case against consciousness in existing architectures

**Matthew Shardlow and Piotr Przybyła (2024) | [Deanthropomorphising NLP](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0307521)**

- **Reasoning:** Their analysis of LaMDA and Transformer-style models through integrated information theory holds that their architecture and prompt-sensitive self-descriptions do not establish subjective experience; fluent dialogue invites anthropomorphic inference.
- **Conditions:** Integrated information theory must correctly specify necessary causal integration, and the architecture analysis must apply to the system in question.
- **Objection:** This is a theory-dependent analysis, not a direct measurement of the absence of experience. Other consciousness theories may disagree.

## Updated 2026 debate: indicators, rival explanation, and calibration

**Butlin, Long et al. | [Identifying Indicators of Consciousness in AI Systems](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011) (online 2025, 2026 volume):** This peer-reviewed account develops their earlier report into an explicit theory-derived indicator method. It improves on taking self-reports literally, but indicators remain indirect, theory-dependent and do not license transferring a verdict on 2023 systems to later architectures.

**Susan Schneider (2026) | [The Error Theory of LLM Consciousness](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X25103920):** Standard LLMs can produce apparently conscious talk by learning concepts and behavioral patterns from human language and feedback, without subjective experience. Schneider regards current LLM self-reports as uninformative about consciousness, while treating bio-computing and neuromorphic systems as distinct candidates. **Objection:** an alternative mechanism for the behavior is not an independent demonstration that every LLM is insentient. This is a peer commentary, not a direct consciousness experiment.

**Florentin Koch (2026) | [Calibration and Transfer in Indicator-Based Assessments](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niag061):** Two separate problems remain: (1) without independently verified artificial consciousness cases, numerical assessments cannot be empirically calibrated; (2) consciousness indicators derived from biology require a cross-substrate *bridge assumption* when applied to distinct artificial causal structures. Comparing digital, neuromorphic, biological and hybrid mechanisms can test the robustness of that transfer but cannot directly supply phenomenal ground truth. **Conclusion:** indicator scores may organize uncertainty, but they are not validated numerical probabilities of AI consciousness.

## Conditional position: triangulate, do not rely on one test

**Butlin et al. (2023); Chalmers (2023)**

- **Reasoning:** Combine indicators derived from multiple theories, observations of behavior, and mechanistic investigation rather than treating self-reports or the label “language model” as decisive.
- **Conditions:** Specify the actual running system and architecture; do not conflate base models, role-play personas, and extended agents.
- **Limit:** The indicators remain indirect. There is no widely accepted decisive test that directly reads another system's subjective experience.

## What the evidence does not settle

These are competing theory-guided and mechanistic arguments, not a settled experimental determination. **Possible consciousness does not establish suffering; lack of demonstrated consciousness does not settle every ethical question about treating AI.**
