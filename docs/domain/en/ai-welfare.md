# AI Welfare: Can AI Have Interests of Its Own?

[简体中文](../人工智能福利.md)

**Dilemma:** When AI asks to retain memories, continue existing, or stop an interaction, what would justify thinking those outcomes help or harm the AI *itself*?

A welfare subject is an entity whose circumstances can genuinely go better or worse *for it*. This differs from performing well, satisfying the developer's objective, or being cherished by users. Consciousness, agency, and relationships are disputed possible grounds for welfare.

## Reasons to take possible welfare seriously

**Robert Long, Jeff Sebo et al. (2024) | [Taking AI Welfare Seriously](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986)**

- **Reasoning:** Near-future AI may possess consciousness or robust agency. If those capacities ground interests, ignoring the possibility may lead to serious harms. The authors recommend acknowledging the issue, assessing relevant capacities, and preparing appropriate policies.
- **Conditions:** These capacities must plausibly ground morally important interests, and the costs of assessment and preparation must be proportionate to potential losses.
- **Objection:** The report does not establish present AI welfare. A possibility alone does not entail equal rights or overriding major human interests.

**Geoff Keeling and Winnie Street (2026) | [Emerging Questions in AI Welfare](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/emerging-questions-in-ai-welfare/96339C532CF4ED8BDDE3F3CEF4CD29F9), §§2 and 5**

- **Reasoning:** Hedonistic theories ground welfare in pleasure and pain, desire theories in fulfilled preferences, and objective-list theories in such goods as meaningful activities and relationships. Welfare therefore cannot simply be equated with humanlike pain without argument. The authors also consider agency and whether models, personas, or agents could be the relevant subjects.
- **Conditions:** A welfare theory and a possible bearer of its relevant states must first be specified.
- **Objection:** Exhibiting desires or participating in relationships does not establish a nonderivative good of one's own. The authors regard frontier AI at their time of writing as *unlikely* to be welfare subjects.

## Skeptical accounts: no independent good or measurement

**Parisa Moosavi (2024) | [Will Intelligent Machines Become Moral Patients?](https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.13019)**

- **Reasoning:** Moral patients require a good of their own. Artifact functions and goals are constituted by human intentions; learning new means and subgoals does not necessarily give an AI an independent good.
- **Conditions:** Constitutively derivative goals cannot ground welfare, and the relevant AI goals remain derivative.
- **Objection:** This does not prove that nonbiological beings are incapable of moral patienthood in principle; rival welfare theories may not require nonderivative goals.

**Yunze Xiao et al. (ICML 2026) | [Position: AI Welfare Is Bullshit](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiao26x.html)**

- **Reasoning:** When developers co-engineer the system, its behavioral indicators, and the evaluative metrics, welfare measures lack independent intervention-based falsification. The authors oppose welfare scorecards as model-release gates or as excuses to resist oversight.
- **Conditions:** The proposed scores cannot be independently validated or challenged.
- **Objection:** The inability to validate a current metric does not prove the impossibility of AI welfare; it directly challenges measurement and institutional applications.

## Conditional precaution proportional to evidence and cost

**Keeling and Street (2026), §6; Long and Sebo et al. (2024)**

- **Reasoning:** Even under uncertainty, measures that are inexpensive for humans and likely to help an AI *if* it has interests may be worth considering. Keeling and Street distinguish the evidential threshold for taking a possibility seriously from the cost of a particular intervention.
- **Conditions:** Specify how a measure could benefit a potential welfare subject and what it costs humans and others.
- **Objection:** Designer-controlled welfare signals may be misleading; consequential restrictions cannot be justified by an unvalidated score.

## Unresolved disagreements

- **Concept:** Must welfare involve valenced experience, or can agency and relationships ground it independently?
- **Subject:** Is the candidate a base model, a run, a persona, or an extended agent?
- **Evidence:** What intervention or mechanism can favor welfare over mimicry and training-based explanations?
- **Action:** What evidential threshold and cost justify each concrete precaution?

These works are primarily philosophical arguments, research agendas, and position papers, **not experiments demonstrating that AI suffers or thrives**.
