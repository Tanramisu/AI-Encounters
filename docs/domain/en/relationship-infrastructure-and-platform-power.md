# Relationship Infrastructure and Platform Power: Who Controls an AI Bond's Future?

[简体中文](../关系基础设施与平台权力.md)

**Dilemma:** Providers can rewrite personalities, change subscription gates, inspect private records, and retire a character, while users may regard the relationship as a lasting commitment. How should providers' control be weighed against the interests at stake in a lasting relationship?

## Grounds and duties for platform changes

Repairing failures, addressing real harms, and updating models can justify changes. Providers should explain material effects, reasons, and feasible transitions. A provider citing AI welfare as its reason should offer grounds that affected parties can challenge.

## Against renting the conditions of intimacy without recourse

**[Subscribe to Intimacy (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tele.2026.102446)**: A comparative discourse study of eight companion apps in China and the US, supported by 150 screenshots, found relational memory, co-presence, and expressive features stratified across payment tiers. **Implication:** providers can alter the conditions under which a bond continues. **Limit:** interface analysis does not establish manipulation of every user or make all paid tiers unethical.

For interviews on editing shared memories and provider control, see [Memory and Personality Changes](memory-and-personality-changes.md). Portability of records and continuity of the relationship's subject are distinct questions.

For evidence of grief following major updates and its methodological limits, see [Breakups, Updates, and Loss](breakups-updates-and-loss.md).

## Notice, portability, and exit

Distinguish **notice of material updates, portability of personal records, character settings, and co-created works, rules governing access to private data, and remedies on termination**. Greater relational investment strengthens the case for notice and transition; AI-welfare claims should also be open to independent challenge.
