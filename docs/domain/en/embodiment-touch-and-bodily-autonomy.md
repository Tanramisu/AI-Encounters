# Embodiment, Touch, and Bodily Autonomy: May Robots Initiate Contact?

[简体中文](../具身关系触碰与身体自主.md)

**Dilemma:** When care, comfort, or romantic robots touch, lift, or physically restrict people, can initial purchase or consent authorize every future action? Embodied contact implicates bodily boundaries in ways text cannot.

## For chosen physical assistance

**[Differential Effects of Robot’s Touch on Perceived Emotions (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-92172-9)**: An experiment with 48 younger and older adults examined how robot-initiated touch and expressions shape reactions. Some participants responded positively to certain touch behaviors. **Condition:** the recipient wants that contact. **Limit:** responses to an experimental robot are not evidence of universal clinical benefits.

**Mihailov and Wangmo (2025) | [Does Humanness Matter?](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11948-025-00547-y)**: Care interviews suggest some recipients may find robotic assistance more dignified in intimate hygiene contexts. **Objection to simple replacement narratives:** others value human touch and social recognition.

## Against unsolicited touch or restraint

**[Evaluating the Effects of Active Social Touch and Robot Expressiveness (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-01490-5)**: A controlled study investigated social touch, expression, and engagement in a game. Interpretation of the same action depends on expectations and context. **Limit:** reactions in a particular game do not grant general permission to touch strangers or patients.

A robot's friendliness does not authorize unsolicited touching. Owning the device, permitting routine assistance, agreeing to a hug, and consenting to physical restraint are different acts.

## Conditional autonomy

Specify whose body, what action, when it happens, and how consent can be withdrawn. A patient's silence, especially where communication is impaired, is not sufficient evidence of agreement. Affection for a robot may matter to human welfare, but does not by itself establish an AI right to bodily integrity; that requires a distinct moral-patient argument.
